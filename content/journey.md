## intro

In Gurbani, every person lives between two ways of facing. The **manmukh** (ਮਨਮੁਖ) is one whose face, *mukh*, is turned towards their own mind, *man*: led by its wants, its fears and its pride. The **gurmukh** (ਗੁਰਮੁਖ) is one whose face is turned towards the Guru: shaped by the Shabad, living in Hukam, remembering Naam.

[[gurbani:gurmukh-sanmukh]]

These are not two kinds of people, sorted once and for all. Gurbani speaks to the manmukh as someone who can turn, and warns the gurmukh against pride. Both ways of facing are present in every life. The journey is the slow turning of a whole person.

[[gurbani:manmukh-bandh]]

This page follows that turning in seven steps. It begins with an honest look at life facing the self, and the moment of turning. Then it follows the map Guru Nanak Dev Ji gives near the close of Japji Sahib: the five *khands*, or realms, of Dharam, Gian, Saram, Karam and Sach. Japji then names the disciplines with which the Shabad is ‘minted’ in a person. Each step links to Babaji's katha of the pauri it draws on, and to the topics where his katha takes it further.

::: tension Is it a ladder?
Commentators read the five khands in different ways. Many teachers expound them as successive stages of spiritual growth, and much katha follows that order. Others read them as dimensions of a single realisation, present together, or as a description of the Divine order rather than of a seeker's progress. Japji itself names the fourth realm *karam*, grace: the decisive step is given, not climbed. This page uses the steps as a map for reflection. It is not a ladder to be scaled by effort, and not a measure for ranking anyone.
:::

## manmukh

**Where everyone begins.** Life facing the self is ordinary; it is where we all start. The mind wants, fears, compares and defends, and we follow it without noticing that we are following anything. Gurbani's portrait of the manmukh is not of a villain. It is of someone asleep: busy, often religious, sometimes successful, and yet turned away from the source of their own life.

[[gurbani:chanan]]

**Its marks.** Gurbani names them. There is haumai, which weighs every experience from the point of view of ‘I’. There are the five thieves of lust, anger, greed, attachment and pride. There is *dūjā bhāu*, the love of ‘the second’, which puts Maya in the place of the One. And there is restlessness, because a mind that is its own master never rests.

[[gurbani:haumai-rog]]

**Why Gurbani names it.** The diagnosis is not meant to condemn. To call haumai an illness is also to say it can be healed, and that the remedy is close at hand.

### In daily life
- Notice how much of a day goes on defending yourself, comparing yourself, or wanting to be seen.
- Notice what you reach for first when you are anxious.

### Reflect
- Where in your life are you most certain that you are right?
- What would you be most afraid to lose tomorrow?

## turning

**The first question.** Japji Sahib opens the journey with a question, not a rule: how does one become truthful, and how is the wall of falsehood brought down? The answer is a direction of walking.

[[gurbani:hukam-rajai]]

**Looking into the mirror.** The turning begins when a person sees their own mind honestly. Guru Amar Das Ji gives the image of a mirror. Mirrors were once polished metal, and rust spoiled the reflection; in the mind, the rust is haumai.

[[gurbani:man-aarsi]]

**Taking the Guru.** No one turns alone. In Gurmat the Guru is the Shabad, met in Gurbani, in katha and in the company of those who live it. One of Babaji's recent divans names this step in its title: ‘ਗੁਰੂ ਧਾਰਨ ਕਰਨਾ’, taking the Guru.

[[gurbani:man-jot-saroop]]

### In daily life
- Keep one fixed time each day for Gurbani, even a few minutes.
- Spend time with the people who make it easier for you to remember.

### Reflect
- When did you last change your mind about something important? What made it possible?
- Who helps you see yourself honestly?

## dharam-khand

**The earth as a place of dharam.** Japji describes creation: nights and seasons, air, water and fire. In the midst of them stands the earth, set in place as a *dharamsāl*, a home where righteous living is practised.

[[gurbani:dharam-sal]]

**Every deed is weighed.** In this realm a person learns that actions have consequences, and that life is lived before a court that is itself true.

[[gurbani:karmi-karmi]]

**What it asks.** The realm of duty is the ground floor of spiritual life: honest earning, keeping one's word, caring for family and community, seva, and the discipline of maryada that gives a day its shape. A spiritual life that tries to skip this realm has nowhere to stand.

### In daily life
- Earn honestly, share some of what you earn, and remember the Giver: *kirat karnī, vaṇḍ chhakṇā, nām japṇā*.
- Keep one small promise this week exactly.

### Reflect
- Which of your responsibilities do you carry grudgingly? What would change if you saw it as dharam?

## gian-khand

**The world grows larger.** In the realm of knowledge the seeker's horizon opens. Japji lists countless winds, waters and fires, countless worlds, teachers and seekers, without end.

[[gurbani:dharam-khand-eho]]

[[gurbani:gian-parchand]]

**Knowledge that humbles.** Spiritual knowledge here is not information. It is the dawning sense of how vast creation is, and how small the ‘I’ is within it. Gurbani is clear that learning which feeds pride only binds a person more tightly.

[[gurbani:path-pario]]

### In daily life
- Read or listen to a little Gurbani each day, with its meaning.
- Notice when knowing more makes you feel above others.

### Reflect
- What have you learned that made you humbler? What has made you prouder?

## saram-khand

**Where a person is reshaped.** Japji says that in this realm attention, understanding, mind and intellect are fashioned anew. This is the work of formation: steady, often hidden, often slow.

[[gurbani:saram-khand]]

[[gurbani:saram-ghariai]]

**Effort, humility, beauty.** The word *saram* is read in more than one way. Many commentators take it as effort or spiritual endeavour; others read it as humility, or as beauty and joy. Each reading catches something true of this realm: it is where patient labour in Naam makes a person beautiful within. Japji's closing Salok calls that labour hard work.

[[gurbani:jini-naam-dhiaya]]

### In daily life
- Keep a daily simran, especially on the days it feels dry.
- Stay close to the sangat; formation happens in company.

### Reflect
- Which practice do you keep only when it feels good?

## karam-khand

**Grace.** The fourth realm is named *karam*, which here means grace, not karma. Its language is power: the power that does what effort alone cannot.

[[gurbani:karam-khand]]

**What changes.** Japji says that this realm is home to spiritual heroes in whom the Divine is present through and through; they do not die and are not deceived. The seeker's part was effort. The completion is a gift.

### In daily life
- Do Ardas: ask, and leave the outcome in the Hukam.
- Notice the help you have received that you did not earn.

### Reflect
- Where are you still trying to earn what can only be received?

## sach-khand

**The realm of truth.** The last realm is the dwelling of the Formless One, who watches over creation with a glance of grace. Japji says that describing it is as hard as steel: words fall short here.

[[gurbani:sach-khand]]

**Not an escape.** The freedom Gurbani speaks of is lived here, in the middle of ordinary life.

[[gurbani:hasandia-khelandia]]

### Reflect
- Have you known moments of complete ease, of *sahaj*? What were you doing?

## mint

Japji then gives the method in a single image: a goldsmith's workshop, in which the Shabad is minted into a human life. Each tool is a quality to be cultivated. Together they describe the gurmukh's discipline.

[[gurbani:taksal]]

[[mint-table]]

The pauri ends where the five khands do, with grace.

[[gurbani:nadar-kar]]

## gurmukh

**The gurmukh.** Gurbani's portrait of the gurmukh is not of someone who has stopped being human. The gurmukh still works, marries, laughs and grieves, but faces the Guru in all of it: speaking from the Shabad, living in Hukam, free of the need to be at the centre.

[[gurbani:gurmukh-mukta]]

[[gurbani:gurmukh-nadam]]

**Further in Babaji's katha.** In Sidh Gosht, Guru Nanak Dev Ji answers the yogis' questions by describing the gurmukh again and again; Babaji has given katha on it. His katha of Japji Sahib follows these pauris in order.
