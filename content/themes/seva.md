## discover

Gurbani does not ask us to leave the world in order to find the Divine. It asks us to serve within it.

[[gurbani:vich-duniya-sev]]

**Seva** (ਸੇਵਾ) is service offered without seeking reward: in the langar, in the gurdwara, in the family and among strangers. In Sikh life it is not an optional good deed added to devotion. It is one of the ways devotion becomes real.

## understand

**Service and remembrance belong together.** Japji Sahib says simply:

[[gurbani:jin-seviya]]

Many commentators read *seviā* here as serving the Divine, which in Gurmat includes remembrance as well as action. The tradition holds the two together: seva without Naam can become busy self-importance, and Naam that never reaches the hands has not yet taken root.

**Honest work and giving.** Gurbani ties seva to the way we earn:

[[gurbani:ghal-khaye]]

This line underlies three familiar Sikh principles: earning honestly (*kirat karnī*), sharing (*vaṇḍ chhakṇā*) and remembrance (*nām japṇā*). Giving is meaningful when it comes from our own honest labour and from our own hand.

**Seva without haumai.** The great danger of service is that it becomes a stage for the ego: being seen to serve, keeping count, expecting gratitude. Sikh tradition remembers Bhai Ghanaiya Ji, who gave water to the wounded of both sides at Anandpur and was blessed by Guru Gobind Singh Ji for seeing the same Light in all. Whatever the details of each telling, the point of the story is clear: real seva makes no distinction between ‘ours’ and ‘theirs’, and expects nothing back.

**In Babaji's katha.** Seva runs through the jeevan katha of the mahapurakhs of this lineage. Sant Jawala Singh Ji's life, as remembered by the Dera, includes building and rebuilding at historic gurdwaras and serving the sangat who came to him. The series on the lives of the Naurangabad mahapurakhs and others (‘Sikh Raaj Ke Usariyo’) tells similar stories of lives given to service. ## reflect

- Whose needs did you meet today without anyone knowing?
- Is there a form of seva you avoid because it seems beneath you?
- Where does your giving come from: duty, habit, guilt, or love?

## practise

This week, do one act of seva that no one will know about, and do not mention it afterwards. Notice what your mind does with the urge to be recognised.
