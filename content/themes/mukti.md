## discover

Many religious traditions place liberation after death or at the end of long withdrawal from the world. Guru Arjan Dev Ji places it somewhere unexpected.

[[gurbani:hasandia-khelandia]]

**Mukti** (ਮੁਕਤੀ) is freedom: from haumai, from the grip of the five thieves, from the cycle of coming and going. Gurbani's distinctive emphasis is *jīvan mukti*, freedom while living, here, in the middle of ordinary life: laughing, playing, dressing and eating.

## understand

**Freedom in the midst of life.** The line does not say freedom comes *despite* ordinary life, or *after* it. It comes *within* it (*viche*). The precondition is meeting the True Guru, through whom ‘the complete way’ (*pūrī jugat*) is found. Mukti is not an escape route; it is a way of living.

**How do we become free?** That is the question in the title of one of Babaji's recent divans: ‘ਮੁਕਤ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਹੋਈਏ’, set beside ‘ਨਾਮ ਦਾ ਭਰੋਸਾ’ (trust in Naam). In Gurmat the path runs through Naam, the Shabad, seva and the dissolving of haumai, and is completed by grace. Gurbani also speaks of the devotee who asks for neither kingdom nor mukti, only love for the Divine; for such a person, love is the freedom.

**Sahaj.** Gurbani often names the liberated state *sahaj*: natural poise, ease, equanimity. It is not trance or withdrawal. Babaji's recent title ‘ਭਜਨ ਅਤੇ ਭੋਜਨ ਸਹਿਜ ਨਾਲ’ (worship and eating, both with ease) catches its flavour: sahaj is how the free person does ordinary things.

::: compare Moksha in Vedanta and in Patanjali's yoga | Vedanta Darshan, Vichar Sagar, Yoga Sutras
Babaji's katha includes talks titled Moksh and Samadhi in his Patanjali series, and long katha on Vedanta texts. In Advaita Vedanta, *moksha* is the realisation that the self was never other than Brahman; in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras the goal is *kaivalya*, the isolation of pure consciousness from the fluctuations of nature. Gurbani shares the longing for release from the cycle of birth and death. It differs in three ways: freedom comes through Naam and grace more than through knowledge or technique; it is lived in society rather than in withdrawal; and it keeps the relationship of love with the Divine rather than dissolving it. Different Sikh commentators weigh these differences differently, and the comparison here is an editorial summary, not a doctrinal ruling.
:::

## reflect

- What are you most afraid of losing? That fear may show you what still binds you.
- Have you known moments of *sahaj*, of being at ease and fully present? What made them possible?
- If freedom is possible while laughing and eating, what is the obstacle in your own laughing and eating?

## practise

Choose one ordinary daily act, eating a meal, walking somewhere, washing up, and do it for a week with full attention and quiet remembrance. Do not try to make it spiritual. Simply be wholly there, and remember the Giver.
