## discover

When the Siddhs asked Guru Nanak Dev Ji who his Guru was, the answer recorded in Sidh Gosht is one of the most radical lines in Gurbani.

[[gurbani:sabad-guru]]

In Gurmat the Guru is not, finally, a body or a personality. It is the **Shabad**, the Divine Word, and the disciple is the attention that listens to it. This is why Sikhs bow to Guru Granth Sahib Ji as the living Guru, and why katha, the careful explanation of Gurbani, has always been central to Sikh life.

## understand

**The Guru as Light and Word.** Sikh tradition holds that the same Light passed through all ten Gurus, and that Guru Gobind Singh Ji placed Guruship in the Granth (and, with it, in the Khalsa Panth). Babaji's katha collection includes his talks at the celebrations of four hundred years of Guru Granth Sahib Ji and a talk titled ‘Guru Manio Granth’. His largest single body of katha, the lareevar katha of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji, follows the Granth Ang by Ang.

**Why the Word needs a listener.** The line says the disciple is *surat dhun*, attention attuned to the Shabad. A book on a shelf transforms no one. The Shabad becomes Guru in a life when it is heard, understood and taken in. That is the purpose of katha and vichar: not to replace Gurbani with commentary, but to help the listener's attention meet it.

**Sangat.** No one walks this path alone. Gurbani defines the true congregation not by place or numbers but by what happens there:

[[gurbani:satsangat-kaisi]]

Babaji's katha returns often to sangat and to the *sadhu* or *sant*, the person in whom the Shabad has become real. One divan from 2009 is titled ‘Characteristics of a weak Sadhu’, and another ‘Sadh Mehima’ (the greatness of the holy). The tradition asks for both reverence and discernment: honour those who live the Shabad, and test every teacher against the Shabad itself.

::: tension Discernment about teachers
Sikhs hold differing views on the place of living sants and deras within Sikh life. Some emphasise their role in keeping Gurbani, Amrit and seva alive across generations; others caution that devotion to a person can compete with the Guru Granth Sahib and the Panth. This site belongs to a dera tradition and does not pretend otherwise; Gurbani, which all sides accept as Guru, remains the measure.
:::

## reflect

- When you hear or read Gurbani, where is your attention: on the sound, the meaning, or somewhere else?
- Which people in your life make it easier for you to remember the Divine? Do you spend time with them?
- How do you tell the difference between respect for a teacher and dependence on one?

## practise

Take a single shabad you already know by heart. Read it slowly with its meaning, or listen to Babaji's katha of the Ang where it appears. Then carry one line of it with you for a week, returning to it whenever you notice it. Let the Shabad do the teaching.
