## discover

Gurbani makes a striking claim about the relationship between spiritual truth and the way we live.

[[gurbani:sach-achar]]

Truth is the highest reality, and yet *truthful living* (*sach āchār*) is placed above it. In Sikh language this is **rahiṇī**: the life that is lived, as distinct from *kahiṇī*, what is merely said. The test of understanding is conduct.

## understand

**Actions have consequences.** Japji Sahib's closing Salok is clear that our deeds matter:

[[gurbani:karmi-apo-apni]]

Babaji's recent divans speak directly of ‘ਕਰਮਾਂ ਦਾ ਹਿਸਾਬ’ (the account of our deeds) and of the fate of the ungrateful. Gurmat holds this alongside Hukam and grace: our actions shape what we become, while grace can still transform us. Neither cancels the other.

**Discipline and maryada.** Living well needs form. Babaji's titles name it plainly: ‘ਮਰਯਾਦਾ ਵਿੱਚ ਰਹਿਣਾ’, staying within the discipline. *Maryādā* is not rigidity for its own sake; it protects attention and character, as banks protect a river.

**Family, marriage and celibacy.** Gurmat honours the householder's life, and Babaji's katha speaks to it directly: a ten-part series on brahmcharya and *pati patni dharam* (the duties of husband and wife), and a seven-part series titled Brahmchari Katha. These are sensitive subjects in which traditional teaching and modern assumptions often meet uneasily; the recordings are best heard in full rather than summarised here.

**Work and the world.** Gurbani asks for honest earning (*kirat*), sharing and remembrance (see the theme on seva). Babaji's katha on Chanakya Niti, the aphorisms on practical and political wisdom attributed to Chanakya, and his talks titled ‘Jeevan Jugtan’ (ways of living) take up the art of acting wisely in a world of power and interests.

::: compare Chanakya's practical wisdom | Chanakya Niti, attributed to Chanakya (Kautilya)
Chanakya Niti offers shrewd counsel on friends and enemies, money, kings and self-protection. Some of it echoes Gurbani's call for discernment and self-discipline. Its frame, however, is prudence and success in the world; Gurmat's frame is Hukam, Naam and truthful living even at cost to oneself. Read through Gurmat, practical wisdom is a tool, not a measure of what is right.
:::

**Living it with others.** Gurbani's most demanding lines are about how we treat people: without slander, without deceit, without pride. Spiritual understanding that leaves relationships untouched has not yet become *rahiṇī*.

## reflect

- Where is the gap widest between what you believe and how you act?
- In your work, what would ‘truthful living’ ask of you this week?
- Which relationship most tests your spiritual life? What is it teaching you?

## practise

Choose one small, concrete commitment for a week: no complaining about absent people, keeping a promised time exactly, or giving a fixed share of a day's earnings. Keep it quietly. At the end of the week, notice what it changed in you.
